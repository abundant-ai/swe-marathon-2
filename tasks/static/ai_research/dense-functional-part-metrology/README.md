# Reconstruct the controls and colored geometry of a real desk

[Strict optical experiment](https://www.oddish.app/experiments/4a694e06) · [Maintained verifier controls](https://www.oddish.app/experiments/801ab77f) · [First clean-source experiment](https://www.oddish.app/experiments/beb3951f) · [Original dense-video model experiment and image ablations](https://www.oddish.app/experiments/726aa958) · [Publication RGB reference and no-op](https://www.oddish.app/experiments/05956d1d) · [Candidate brief](instruction.md)

A researcher wants to project a cursor onto a particular gamepad button as a handheld camera moves around a cluttered desk. The agent writes a Python program that returns a colored metric triangle mesh. Ten face labels distinguish the eight gamepad control surfaces, the blue annulus of the red desktop device and the exposed front seal of a carton. Assigning a close-fitting shell to a whole controller leaves the individual controls unresolved.

## Input and deliverable

Workspace A comes from the TUM Freiburg 1 desk recording. The agent receives a contiguous 2.4-second video spanning 73 original 640 × 480 RGB exposures, the corresponding native PNG sequence, per-frame tracked optical poses, camera intrinsics and six calibrated keyframes from an adjacent interval. The camera traveled 1.27 m during the continuous clip. The final frame of that video is also the first public keyframe. Both public inputs exclude every withheld evaluation photograph. The nearest dense exposure to a withheld photograph is 264 ms away. The agent can examine pixels, fit geometry numerically, run its complete program, and correct its source after the public smoke test. Geometry must fit within 20,000 triangles. ## Measurements

| Component | Measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Ten functional surfaces | Render the nearest labeled triangle at every native pixel through the calibrated, distorted RGB lens. Compare it with the independent visible footprint. For each part and view, count thresholds passed from 0.50 to 0.95 in 0.05 steps, then average across visible views and equally across parts. The four photographs contain 38 usable part footprints. |
| Three parent objects | On measured Kinect rays, compare object IDs and predicted depth. A correctly labeled hit within 3 cm contributes to F1. Pool four views separately for each object, then take the three-object mean. |
| Colored workspace | For every held-out view, measure the fraction of valid depth pixels whose visible mesh surface is within 5 cm and whose interpolated sRGB color has mean absolute error at most 25.5 levels. Average the four fractions equally. Missing depth contributes no evidence. |

The reward is the smallest component. Each report also retains the raw mask IoU, strict threshold score, individual part results, per-object measurements and depth-only workspace coverage. Before the first functional-part trial, a duplicate trace of one photograph showed that one native pixel can materially change the score of a small, blurred button. The primary footprint comparison requires coverage of the mask interior after one-pixel erosion and penalizes predictions beyond one-pixel dilation. Repeated traces had threshold agreement 0.611 using the unadjusted masks and 0.911 with the declared one-pixel band. Two authoring agents traced real RGB independently of the submitted meshes. This was a single-view repeatability check, without human inter-rater measurement. Coarse depth uses the nominal Kinect registered-depth projection. This desk pilot asks a coding agent to produce a scene program and uses held-out photographs to measure the shapes and identities of ten contact surfaces. Its Kinect depths provide much sparser evidence on individual buttons than a dedicated surface scan would. ## Feasibility and sensitivity

The RGB-only control triangulates author-traced contact surfaces across the public keyframes, fits their shapes and adds them to an earlier source-produced RGB scene. Its contextual mesh was derived from a previous, less detailed task on the same recording. A separate reference uses input-time sensor depth for its supporting surfaces and the same RGB-fitted parts. The original model-run bundle shipped that depth-assisted mesh as its privileged oracle. The maintained publication instead rebuilds the RGB-only scene from source. Neither fit uses withheld RGB outlines to place the controls, and neither demonstrates an automated end-to-end solution on a new recording.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fitted RGB-only scene | 0.670 | 0.746 | 0.732 | 0.670 |
| Input-depth shell and RGB-fitted parts | 0.670 | 0.856 | 0.682 | 0.670 |

A depth shell without the part labels scores zero on fine geometry. Keeping the parts but placing them on one camera-facing plane yields 0.075. Cyclically permuting the eight gamepad labels yields 0.190. The upper bound remains unknown. [Geometry controls and source](scripts/evidence/functional-controls.json)

To test which positioning errors the metric detects, we moved the eight gamepad control surfaces in the RGB reference, holding the shell, colors, ring and carton seal fixed. A 5 mm sideways shift along each control plane lowered the full score from 0.670 to 0.228 or 0.240, depending on direction. Raising all eight surfaces 5 mm toward the cameras lowered it to 0.408. Sinking them by the same amount gave 0.603, still above the experimental difficulty threshold. A 10 mm normal displacement gave 0.335 in both directions. These perturbations quantify sensitivity around the fitted reference. The sparse depth samples leave the physical height of individual buttons uncertain. ## Public-data reference implementation

The publication oracle measures the physical desk edges in the supplied RGB images, fits the desk coordinate frame from their tracked cameras, and rebuilds the contextual surfaces and colors. For the controls, it triangulates the hand-traced public centers and contours through the calibrated native lens, fits planar conics across the input views, and removes intersecting shell triangles before attaching the labeled faces. It samples the continuous video for exposed control colors and encodes the complete program into the same source artifact a candidate submits. The numerical mesh contains 19,979 triangles. Local reconstruction reached 0.670 on contacts, 0.746 on parent depth and 0.732 on the colored workspace. A three-pixel horizontal shift of the six keyframes moved the re-estimated desk origin by 4.72 mm with the pose rows held fixed. The solution carries the original public-view feature picks and parametric shape assumptions. It contains no saved mesh, target-scene depth map or held-out RGB outline. The source traces describe this particular desk and would require relabeling for another recording. ## Task design and limits

| Dimension | Evidence and uncertainty |
| --- | --- |
| Novelty | 3DCodeFactory's Baroque Hall of Mirrors calls for an interactive scene from a reference and explicit structural specifications. BlenderGym's object placement uses a base Blender scene and goal render. Here the metric placement and visible contact surfaces must be inferred from a real recording with independent held-out physical evidence. Within this repository, the earlier clutter task measured three object shells and the six-keyframe task added contact labels without full colored-scene coverage. Coverage across all external SWE collections is unverified. |
| Verifiability | Independent optical contours, registered depths, repeated labeling of one view and explicit wrong controls measure different defects. Reflections, coarse depth samples and annotation disagreement limit precision. Surfaces wholly invisible to all withheld cameras remain unscored. |
| Realism | An editable metric scene supports a researcher projecting targets onto physical controls. Camera calibration, occluders and changes in viewpoint are measured. The labels are specific to one desk, and the 45-minute candidate window does not establish a five-day research workload. |
| Difficulty | Models must locate individual controls under changing perspective, put them at the correct metric depth and preserve full-scene appearance. Correctly identifying the controller and reconstructing its parent surface can leave individual button footprints misplaced. Three matching GPT trials stayed below 0.5. |
| Initial feasibility | Multiview triangulation and shape fitting produce an RGB-only example above 0.5. The input-depth control provides a second context reconstruction. Both share the same manually placed part fits. Generalization to new scenes remains open. |

## Packaging and verification

The authoring tree contains a maintained verifier. Its bounded loader checks NPY headers, numeric shape, type, indices and declared expanded size before allocating the mesh. A dedicated worker UID, resource limits and cleanup cover the submitted source and its children. A fresh evaluation clears old result files, distinguishes a candidate contract failure from an infrastructure failure, and reports each requirement independently. The 0.5 per-component status in that report is descriptive. Continuous geometry determines reward. A receipt documents the source it names and can be fabricated by a candidate. Trajectory evidence and independent reconstruction strengthen the claim that the reported programs ran. Both agent and maintained deployment environments include ffmpeg. The 600-second CPU rlimit applies per process. The build also has a 640-second elapsed-time limit. The private worker has a dedicated UID with a 32-process/thread limit. The public PRoot check runs under the shared authoring UID and omits that per-UID limit. Aggregate CPU usage across descendants is unmeasured. The frozen version-1 verifier supplied the PNG archive and omitted ffmpeg from its replay image.

The hosted model experiment used the original version-1 verifier. The maintained source changes worker handling and public limits, reinstates the public observations under their absolute deployment paths, checks all 38 native footprint denominators and the depth counts for every held-out view, and preserves the numerical scoring implementation. An archive replay of the nine GPT meshes reproduced every compared component to within 6 × 10⁻¹⁷. A local resource-limited bootstrap rebuilt the three matching programs, preserving all triangle indices and label arrays exactly. Maximum coordinate drift from the hosted meshes was 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ m. Local process-group cleanup, malformed numerical data, fresh-state and candidate/infrastructure classification checks passed. The clean-source public runner stages only the submitted Python and released observations into a fresh /app/work plus a fresh temporary directory. It preserves the writer's files and resolves absolute, current-directory and source-relative paths identically to deployment.
