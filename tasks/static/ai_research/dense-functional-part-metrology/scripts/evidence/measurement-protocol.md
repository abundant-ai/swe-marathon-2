# Functional-part reconstruction from the Freiburg desk recording

## Protocol fixed before running a successor agent

The previous matching-A screen reached 0.8021 on three broad object surfaces. The gamepad earned 0.915 for depth agreement within 3 cm. The successful program modeled the controller shell, two sticks and four buttons. Its score did not distinguish an individually usable button surface from a similarly placed piece of housing. The successor asks for the physical footprints of ten named parts as additional face labels on the full scene mesh.

We resolve the nearest triangle at each 640 × 480 native pixel from four tracked withheld RGB camera poses. For each visible, independently annotated part and view, compute the intersection over union of the two native masks. Report the proportion of thresholds passed from 0.50 through 0.95 in steps of 0.05. Average each part across its available views, then each of the ten parts equally. This uses the familiar COCO overlap thresholds without confidence ranking, since each physical part occurs once. The blue annulus mask includes the negative space of its center and any visible occluder. Dropping an unobservable or heavily clipped part from one photograph does not drop its other views. The final reward is the smaller of this part score and the original three-object 3 cm surface F1. Both fine control geometry and the complete workspace matter. Also report raw part IoUs, original object scores, and the independent annotators' agreement. No cutoff will be tuned to agent scores.

The calibration mesh uses optical parts independently drawn on the public keyframes and fitted through the public camera rays. Its contextual shell is the existing privileged two-input RGB-D control. We report the contribution of the privileged inputs separately. A one-camera billboard with correct input silhouettes, a cap-ID permutation and an empty-part shell test depth, part identity and coarse geometry. Independent duplicate labels on one withheld view estimate the granularity the imagery supports. The evaluator does not infer millimeter-scale shape from blurred RGB or treat the apparent circular red center as a physical hole in the housing.

## Calibration amendment before any successor agent

In a blind duplicate outline of held-out image 2, the nine visible optical surfaces agreed at raw IoUs of 0.72–0.88. Only 0.611 of the COCO thresholds were reached despite the independent identification of the same parts. The input-fitted control had 0.696 mean raw IoU and reached 0.445 of thresholds. The annotation audit identified 1–2 native pixels of uncertainty for the small blurred caps. The primary calculation now excludes one four-neighbor pixel on both sides of the reference edge before counting native mask intersection and union. The core inside the eroded reference remains mandatory. Predictions farther than one pixel beyond the reference count against precision. This fixed one-pixel optical uncertainty applies to every part and view. We retain the exact raw IoU and raw threshold score alongside it. Coarse depth uses the predecessor's documented registered-depth grid. The native optical masks use the separately calibrated Freiburg 1 RGB lens and tracked RGB exposure poses. The camera parameters are public. [TUM RGB-D calibration](https://cvg.cit.tum.de/data/datasets/rgbd-dataset/file_formats)

The ten overlap thresholds follow COCO's documented default. This task evaluates one known instance per ID and macro-averages threshold successes without ranking detections by confidence.
