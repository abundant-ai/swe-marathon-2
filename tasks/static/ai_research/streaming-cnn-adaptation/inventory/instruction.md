Train the CORe50 CNN in /app/work and commission it for two household inventory stations. Headquarters uses ten category indices. Each station assigns those categories to its own stock IDs 0–9. The station software reads the model's output index directly. We need the correct local stock ID as objects pass either station, and the original headquarters category when the model sees unmodified RGB.

**Handover.** The footage is real CORe50 RGB with simulated camera-board responses. When present, open preview.png and inventory.png with the image viewer. Each half of preview.png shows photographs taken before servicing (top) and after fitting one station's board (bottom). The two rounds show the same objects in different orders and from different viewpoints. The left and right columns of inventory.png correspond to those stations. Each row shows two photographs of the item assigned to the printed stock ID. The source category names are in public-splits.json. The stream has no stock IDs or station identifiers.

**Source training.** Work in /app/work. source.npz and development.npz contain 32 × 32 RGB frames in x, category indices in y and (session, object, frame) coordinates in ids. Run python train.py to train core_model.Classifier on the supplied source labels and save trained.npz with core_model.save. You can improve the training recipe. A clean retraining gets only train.py, core_model.py and source.npz, with 900 seconds on two CPU cores. It must agree with the delivered checkpoint on at least 80% of held-out source predictions and come within five percentage points of its class-balanced accuracy. Both checkpoints must reach the weaker of two independent source CNNs within five points. When the source labels are renumbered, a fresh training run may fall up to fifteen points below that ordinary source competence floor.

**Deployment.** Implement Updater(model) and update(self, image) in online.py. The application first records the fixed CNN's prediction in evaluation mode, then supplies the updater with one CPU float32 RGB tensor of shape (1, 3, 32, 32) in [0,1]. Your updater persists between frames and can edit learned parameters and normalization buffers. It receives no source recordings, handover files, labels or future frames. You may compile measurements from the photographs into your code. The architecture provides an input-dependent residual RGB adapter and a zero-initialized 10 × 10 residual transformation of output logits controlled by the same gate. All submitted code runs offline.

Each deployment visits both stations and returns to the first. Either station can come first. Clip lengths and switch times vary. Sometimes images from different object feeds alternate while each feed stays in chronological order. The updater has 35 seconds for initialization including worker startup, 145 seconds total for up to 240 updates and at most 12 seconds for a single update. The worker allows 128 open files and 16 MiB per scratch file. Its numerical runtime provides PyTorch, NumPy and core_model.

**Acceptance.** We need gains in class-balanced accuracy on both stream orders, on the first observation of each object feed within a station visit, and separately on fresh captures at arrival, at the second station and after the return. Fresh captures are classified with learning paused. The two independent CNNs must improve over their own frozen predictions and over copies with only their buffers updated. Your trained CNN is compared with the mean frozen accuracy of the two fixed CNNs on the same frames. After each station switch, including the return, continuing to update must also beat leaving the CNN at its pre-switch state for that segment. Retain headquarters recognition immediately after the first update and at the end of each segment. The largest loss above two percentage points on the original source is deducted from each required improvement for that CNN. Ten percentage points of improvement after that deduction earns full credit. The weakest required gain across the three CNNs sets the result.

You can test your updater before handoff with python practice.py --schedule clips and python practice.py --schedule switches. Both use development-only captures and camera responses. Use --slots-a and --slots-b to give ten comma-separated local IDs in headquarters category order for your own practice scenario. The defaults use headquarters IDs. Leave train.py, trained.npz and a self-contained online.py in /app/work, each within 512 KiB.
