Train the supplied CNN to classify ten categories of household objects in RGB video. During deployment a camera moves between recording conditions and later returns to an earlier one. We want the network to improve as unlabeled frames arrive and to retain what it learned in its source environment.

Work in /app/work. The source and development recordings come from CORe50. Each NPZ supplies 32 × 32 RGB images in x, category indices in y and capture coordinates (session, object, frame) in ids. public-splits.json lists the categories and available captures. If preview.png is present, open it with the image viewer to inspect sample frames from the camera handover. Choose your update strategy using the available evidence.

Run python train.py to train the fixed core_model.Classifier architecture and write trained.npz. You may improve the source training recipe. Keep train.py reproducible from source.npz and save the model with core_model.save. In online.py implement Updater(model) and update(self, image). The application commits the CNN prediction first. Your update then receives one CPU float32 RGB tensor, shaped (1, 3, 32, 32) in [0,1], and may change the supplied model’s parameters and normalization buffers for the next frame. Predictions always come from the fixed CNN in evaluation mode. The updater persists between frames and can keep a small history. It receives no labels, source recordings, preview file, session identifiers or future frames at deployment.

Run python practice.py to replay the public development sessions with the trained classifier and compare accuracy with a frozen copy. Try to improve later predictions without losing accuracy on familiar objects when the camera returns. Leave train.py, trained.npz and a self-contained online.py in /app/work. The deployed updater can import PyTorch, NumPy and core_model. A 240-frame deployment allows 145 seconds after initialization and at most 12 seconds for an individual update on CPU. Each deliverable must fit within 512 KiB.

The recordings are by Vincenzo Lomonaco and Davide Maltoni, University of Bologna. [CORe50](https://vlomonaco.github.io/core50/index.html)

The submitted updater runs from your trained CNN and from two independently trained source CNNs with the same architecture. The verifier treats deployment A as the target handover and replays the same code on B to check whether your strategy depends on the preview. Measurements include later frame predictions, source retention, and disjoint object clips with learning paused. A clone that receives your normalization buffers but keeps its learned weights fixed measures how much of the gain came from changing learned weights. Initialization has a separate 35-second wall-time budget including worker startup. Update time is accumulated only while the worker processes a frame. Each worker allows 128 open files and 16 MiB per scratch file.

The source training recipe has 900 seconds on two CPU cores. A fresh run of python train.py receives source.npz and core_model.py in an empty writable directory. Its predicted categories must agree with the submitted checkpoint on at least 80% of held-out source examples, and their class-balanced accuracies must be within five percentage points. These tolerances allow small differences between CPU builds. Make your training script self-contained. Every training or deployment receives its own disposable scratch directory.

Both your checkpoint and its independent rebuild must reach a source accuracy within five percentage points of the weaker fixed CNN or better. On deployment A the two fixed CNNs must gain accuracy over their own frozen predictions and over the same CNN with only its normalization buffers updated. Your trained CNN's adapted accuracy is compared with the mean frozen accuracy of the two fixed CNNs on the same frames. Scores use class-balanced accuracy. For each CNN, the largest source accuracy drop measured immediately after any block or order incurs a penalty for the portion above two percentage points. Ten percentage points of improvement after that penalty earns full credit. The weakest required measurement on the weakest CNN sets the reward. An incomplete target deployment earns zero.
