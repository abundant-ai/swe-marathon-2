Two replacement camera boards send images through the same service. Each board mixes the sensor color channels differently. During a deployment the service changes boards and later returns to the first one. The order, clip lengths and switch times are unknown to the updater. Original RGB recordings must remain recognizable. The accuracy check runs immediately after the first update on each new camera segment and again at the end of that segment. The footage is real CORe50 video with simulated board responses.

When supplied, preview.png has two side-by-side panels. Each panel shows the same ten exposures before servicing (top) and after passing through one board (bottom). The board order in the sheet is independent of the deployment order. At authoring time you can inspect the sheet, experiment on public captures and compile calibration constants into your updater. At deployment the fixed CNN includes an optional input-conditioned 3 × 3 residual color adapter, initialized as the identity transformation. You may edit its learned parameters and those of the backbone. The verifier pauses learning to measure accuracy on fresh objects from each camera. Fresh objects on the intervening camera and on the returning camera each count toward the reward. A separate diagnostic freezes the CNN after its first camera segment and reports what subsequent updates contributed on the identical later frames.

Run python practice.py --schedule clips and python practice.py --schedule switches to test the same program on two development-only board responses and inspect original-RGB retention.
