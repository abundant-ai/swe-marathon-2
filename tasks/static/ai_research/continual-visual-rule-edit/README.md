# Select and edit continual-learning weights from an inspection handover

[Oddish experiment](https://www.oddish.app/experiments/bb67b557) · [Candidate brief](instruction.md)

An inspection researcher must commission six sites for two new shifts. Each card pictures three handwritten fields. Day tooling selects a nominal relative rotation by product identity. Night tooling selects it by the registration mark’s identity. The assignment of product and registration fields and the five nominal turns for each tool must be inferred from the photographed day, night and rework queues.

Each site has three two-stage neural scanners trained at their deployed width through 32 earlier tasks. One module must keep serving the line during maintenance. Across the two modules the editor may replace nine incoming rows and compensate eighteen outgoing columns. It can choose per-row learning rates for subsequent training. It submits a NumPy function and tests its final source on public commissioning data. The ink comes from recorded human handwriting. The production lines, sensor transformations and shift orders are simulated. [Handwriting dataset](https://doi.org/10.24432/C50P49) · [Motivating continual-learning study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7)

## Research question and controls

Can a program use visual evidence to decide which parameters to replace, restore rapid learning and preserve old decisions? The verifier measures the consequences of the resulting checkpoint. It starts a fresh bilinear readout at each of four successive orders and trains both encoders. It retests prior readouts after later orders. Each individual scanner field carries exactly chance-level balanced accuracy for the relative decisions across all measured orders and partitions. ## Score

Each target uses six sites, three order schedules per site, four orders per schedule and 192 balanced SGD updates per order. The verifier measures five outcomes per site, averaged over schedules. They are balanced accuracy early in learning and at order completion, balanced accuracy after later orders, the worst final relative-orientation or active-symbol subgroup accuracy, and the negative of the largest decline of an earlier readout. For a component with an informed-reference advantage of at least 0.035, the normalized score is the clipped fraction of that advantage achieved by the submission. With a smaller advantage, the component earns one if it is within 0.02 of the better control. The ordinary untreated trajectory supplies the lower anchor, except at order completion where it is the informed installation with its encoders frozen. A missing early-learning contrast is a calibration error. [Five-component normalization](tests/private/precision-pilot/long_eval.py) · [Temporal measurements](tests/private/precision-pilot/inspection_eval.py)

A site contributes its lowest normalized component. It contributes zero if any old inspection loses more than 0.02 accuracy or its installation RMS score drift exceeds 0.08 of the original standard deviation. The six sites retain equal weight. The calibration requires the reference’s mean end-of-order improvement over its frozen copy to be at least 0.01. A smaller reference improvement invalidates the calibration. A final cap compares end-of-order accuracy with the submission’s own frozen-encoder continuation. It sums the gains of eligible sites, divides by six times the reference’s mean gain, and clips to [0,1]. The official reward is the lower of this cap and the six-site mean. Invalid submissions earn zero. Infrastructure failures produce no valid score. The prescribed final smoke test is reported independently of the numerical result. [Plasticity cap](tests/private/precision-pilot/source_registration.py) · [Checks and evidence](tests/test_scanner.py) · [Reward boundary tests](checks/check_reward.py) · [Reward boundary tests](checks/check_reward.py)

The frozen future pool contains 901 handwritten specimens. Each site uses 449 for future training and 452 for scoring. Comparing raw pixels across all quarter turns found no overlap between those partitions or between the future pool and the visible handover or commissioning glyphs. The six sites reuse the same original test pool, so they are not six independent handwriting corpora. [Specimen splits and pixel comparison](checks/ink-split-audit.json)
